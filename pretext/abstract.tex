\dresumoheader{mieiro}{Manoel Jorge de Lima}{The Challenge of the Expository Method: Monitoring Student Attention and Engagement in Remote Classes with the ESPEON Software}{2025}{Undergraduate Thesis}{Federal Center for Technological Education Celso Suckow da Fonseca}{Rio de Janeiro}{Rio de Janeiro}{2025}

This study analyzes the level of distraction and low engagement observed in classes exposed to expository lectures in a remote learning environment. To support the investigation, a Web browser extension called ESPEON was developed to actively monitor student behavior during classes by recording navigation data and the use of input peripherals (camera and microphone). The research was conducted with three undergraduate classes in Information Systems at the Federal Center for Technological Education Celso Suckow da Fonseca (CEFET/RJ), Maria da Graça campus. Students attended expository lectures through the Microsoft Teams platform, using the extension passively. At the end of each session, the tool generated reports with metrics indicative of student attention and interactivity. Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and cross-referenced with qualitative questionnaire responses. The results indicate satisfactory levels of concentration throughout the classes but low engagement, particularly in multimedia activities. In the self-assessment questionnaires, students reported good concentration capacity and evaluated the applied expository methodology positively, highlighting the simultaneous use of multiple screens and mobile devices as the main sources of distraction.
